Executive summaryThe answer, up front

The project asked whether NFL scoring discrepancies can appear after a game looks finished, whether they can cross a market-relevant threshold, and whether this can be detected automatically. Here is what the evidence says.

A game's final score is effectively immutable once posted. Across — already-completed games compared between four historical snapshots and today, not one score, result, total or overtime flag was ever revised. Game totals, spreads and team totals are therefore not materially exposed to post-game corrections.

What does change is attribution and statistics — and it changes every week. A receiver's 44 yards becomes 43. A sack is reassigned. A play is reclassified from a forward pass to a backwards lateral. A player recorded as not having played at all is credited with three catches for 36 yards, four days later. Those move player props and fantasy scoring, and they are routine.

The official feed that used to publish these was switched off in 2026. fantasy.nfl.com/research/statcorrections — the page that carried "official stat corrections as released by the NFL League Office and the official statistician of the NFL, Elias Sports Bureau" — now 302-redirects to a news hub that promotes ESPN as the official fantasy partner. Archived snapshots survive and are what this database is built from, but live alerting can no longer rely on an authoritative feed. See Limitations.

Finding 4Exposure — which games could have flipped

The brief asks us to separate corrections that actually changed an outcome from corrections that merely had the potential to change a market. The first half is answered above (zero score revisions). This is the second half: how close the finished games actually came to a market line.

Read this as sensitivity, not occurrence. A row means a scoring correction would have changed a market. Finding 5Can this be detected automatically?

Partly — and it is important to be precise about which part. Detection is solved and shipped. Attribution — knowing a detected change is a genuine official Elias correction rather than a data-provider glitch — is not solvable for free in 2026.

How the engine works, and why it is built this way

Since there is no authoritative feed to poll, the system keeps its own versioned snapshots of a mirror of the official record and diffs them. Three rules — each one derived from a real bug found by testing against real data — keep the signal clean:

  • Only compare records already final at the older snapshot. A naive diff flagged 61 "changes"; all 61 were simply games that had not kicked off yet.
  • Only compare frozen fields — score, result, total, overtime. Pre-game betting lines move constantly; 18 of those 61 false positives were pure line movement.
  • Carry both values and SHA-256 hashes of both inputs, so any alert can be independently reproduced by a third party.

The machine detects; a human confirms. Every alert is emitted as detected_by_diff_pending_manual_confirmation with actually_changed_outcome: null. A test in the suite fails the build if any record ever asserts a realised outcome change without evidence. The system points at a discrepancy — it does not certify one.

The official record and who controls it

The official corrections page stated its own provenance, quoted verbatim:

"View official stat corrections as released by the NFL League Office and the official statistician of the NFL, Elias Sports Bureau."

So Elias Sports Bureau is the official statistician of the NFL, the NFL League Office co-publishes, and that publication is what controls the final record. That channel is now retired.

What this project deliberately does not do
  • No fabricated prop lines. We model the pricing convention of line-priced stats. We never assert that a line existed at a specific number.
  • No realised-outcome claims. Every record documents potential impact only. A test fails the build if an outcome change is asserted without evidence.
  • No unverifiable anecdote. Widely repeated community reports of corrections flipping fantasy championships are excluded, because they could not be verified to primary-source standard. Their absence is a known gap, not evidence that nothing happened.
  • No inflating the row count. The database is a verified seed, not the full archived corpus. 36 checkable rows beat thousands nobody can verify.
