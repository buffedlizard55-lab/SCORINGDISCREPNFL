01

Corrections happen after games end

The NFL’s GSIS material says game information comes from its statistical system and that existing information is corrected regularly with Elias Sports Bureau. AP reporting describes weekly Wednesday review, later team requests, no fixed deadline, and some October/November statistics still being changed in January/February.

There is no measured correction rate yet

The nine game cases below are deliberately curated examples, not a random sample or complete season-by-season scrape. Seven cited report/table dates fall 2–4 days after the game; the Caleb Williams change was reported 65 days later, and the AP Rodgers article appeared 76 days after its game but does not reveal the NFL correction date. Source-date types vary, so this is not a reliable frequency distribution.

03

Official records and settlement rules differ

The NFL statistical record controls the official statistic; each operator’s applicable, jurisdiction-specific rules govern wager settlement. Fanatics’ current Tennessee rules describe a settlement window and possible amendments, while ESPN and Fleaflicker document separate fantasy cutoffs. One provider’s policy cannot be generalized to another operator, state, or market.

How to read the ledger

Method & safeguards

Evidence grades

  • ADirect correction record. An official NFL Fantasy correction row states the before/after values; final NFL stats/game center corroborate the result.
  • BCorroborated contemporaneous sources. Initial values and the correction are documented in dated team, platform, or media reports; no primary correction artifact was found.
  • CIncomplete or reconstructed. A secondary report supports the change, but one value or the actual correction date is inferred or unavailable.

What counts as “actual market impact”?

It requires a time-stamped, archived market line, the applicable operator/jurisdiction settlement rule, and a documented final disposition. A stat changing across a hypothetical threshold is marked only as possible impact. NFL Fantasy points or a fantasy-platform decision are not sportsbook evidence.

Controlling records

Use NFL GSIS / the NFL’s official statistician (Elias) for the official statistical record; use current official player logs and game centers to corroborate final values and score. A gamebook is a valuable official snapshot, but its initial publication is not a version-history log. A wager’s operator rules—not the NFL or fantasy-provider cutoff—determine the sportsbook settlement.

“Own the Outcome” checklist

  1. 01 Preserve a contemporaneous original box score or feed snapshot.
  2. 02 Capture the correction source, reported date, and exact play/reason if available.
  3. 03 Verify the current official player log, gamebook, score, and winner independently.
  4. 04 Match only market types whose settlement definitions cover that statistic.
  5. 05 Find an archived book line and rule before recording actual betting impact.
